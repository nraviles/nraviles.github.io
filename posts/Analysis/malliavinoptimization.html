Initial Example and Calculations

To illustrate the framework, consider approximating a target process such as Brownian motion \(W_t\) over a time horizon \([0, T]\). Define the objective functional:

\[ \mathcal{J}[X(\sigma)] = \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^T (W_t^2 - X_t)^2 \mathrm{d}t\right] \]

which measures the expected squared deviation between \(X_t\) and the path \(W_t^2\).

Suppose \(X_t\) belongs to a class of pure diffusion processes:

\[ X_t = X_0 + \int_0^t \sigma(s,X_s) \mathrm{d}W_s \]

We examine the first variation \(\delta \mathcal{J}\) under a perturbation \(\sigma \mapsto \sigma + \varepsilon \gamma\), where \(\gamma_t\) is a suitably regular stochastic process vanishing on boundaries:

\[ \begin{align*} \delta \mathcal{J}[X(\sigma)] &= \lim_{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^T (W_t^2 - X_t + \varepsilon \tilde{X}_t)^2 \mathrm{d}t\right] - \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^T (W_t^2 - X_t)^2 \mathrm{d}t\right] \right) \\ &= \mathbb{E}\left[ \int_0^T 2(W_t^2 - X_t)\tilde{X}_t \mathrm{d}t \right] \end{align*} \]

Expanding the variation process \(\tilde{X}_t\):

\[ \delta \mathcal{J}[X(\sigma)] = \mathbb{E}\left[ \int_0^T 2(W_t^2 - X_t) \left(\int_0^t \gamma(s,X_s) \mathrm{d}W_s \right) \mathrm{d}t \right] \]

To express this variation as an inner product, we introduce the Skorohod integral operator \(\delta(\cdot)\), formally written using white noise \(\dot{W}_s\) and Wick product \(\diamond\) (Gjessing et al. 1992):

\[ \delta(\cdot) = \int_0^T (\cdot)\diamond \dot{W}_s \mathrm{d}s = \int_0^T (\cdot) \mathrm{d}W_s \]

Using the Malliavin integration-by-parts duality under Gaussian measure (Nualart 2019):

\[ \mathbb{E}\left[ F \cdot \delta(u) \right] = \mathbb{E}\left[ \int_0^T (\mathbf{D}_s F) u_s \mathrm{d}s \right] = \mathbb{E}\left[ \langle\mathbf{D}_s F, u \rangle_{[0,T]} \right] \]

where \(\mathbf{D}_s\) denotes the Malliavin derivative operator. Differentiating diffusion processes yields:

\[ \mathbf{D}_s X_T = \mathbf{D}_s \int_{0}^T \mu(r,X_r) \mathrm{d}r = \int_{s}^T \mathbf{D}_s \mu(r,X_r) \mathrm{d}r \]

\[ \mathbf{D}_s X_T = \mathbf{D}_s \int_{0}^T \sigma(r,X_r) \mathrm{d}W_r = \sigma(s,X_s) + \int_{s}^T \mathbf{D}_s \sigma(r,X_r) \mathrm{d}W_r \]

For monomial path evaluations \(W_t^n\):

\[ \mathbf{D}_s (W_t^n) = n W_t^{n-1} \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s) \]

Rewriting \(\tilde{X}_t = \delta\left(\mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}\gamma\right)\), the functional derivative becomes:

\[ \begin{align*} \delta \mathcal{J} &= \int_0^T 2\mathbb{E}\left[ \int_0^T \mathbf{D}_s (W_t^2 - X_t) \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s)\gamma(s,X_s) \mathrm{d}s\right] \mathrm{d}t \\ &= \int_0^T 2\mathbb{E}\left[ \int_0^T (2 W_t \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s) - \mathbf{D}_s X_t) \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s)\gamma(s,X_s) \mathrm{d}s\right] \mathrm{d}t \\ &= \int_0^T 2\mathbb{E}\left[ \langle 2 W_t \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s) - \mathbf{D}_s X_t, \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}\gamma \rangle\right] \mathrm{d}t \end{align*} \]

Stationarity requires:

\[ \mathbf{D}_s X_t = \sigma(s,X_s) + \int_s^t \mathbf{D}_s\sigma(r,X_r)\mathrm{d}W_r = 2 W_t \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s) \]

Note that setting \(\sigma(s,X_s) = 2 W_s \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s)\) formally satisfies this relation, but makes \(\sigma\) explicitly path-dependent via \(W_s\) rather than a deterministic state function \(\sigma(s,x)\).

Polynomial & Hermite Expansions

When restricting \(X_t\) to finite Hermite polynomial expansions:

\[ X_t = \sum_{n=0}^N c_n H_n \left(\frac{W_t}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \]

the Malliavin derivative simplifies to:

\[ \mathbf{D}_s X_t = \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{c_n}{\sqrt{t}} H_{n-1} \left(\mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s)\frac{W_t}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \]

Since Hermite polynomials form an orthonormal basis on Wiener space (Gjessing et al. 1992; Nualart 2019), the coefficients satisfy:

\[ c_n = \langle F, H_n\rangle_{\Omega} = \mathbb{E}[F H_n] = \mathbb{E}[\partial^n_x F] \]

When coefficients are time-dependent (\(c_n \doteq c_n(t)\)), the Skorohod integral takes the Wick product form:

\[ \delta\left(c_n(t) H_n \left(\frac{W_t}{\sqrt{t}}\right) \right) = \int_0^T \left(\frac{c_n(t)}{t^{n/2}}W^{\diamond n}_t\right) \diamond \dot{W}_t \mathrm{d}t \]

For general diffusions with drift \(\mu(r,X_r)\) and volatility \(\sigma(r,X_r)\), the tangent variation process \(Y_r = \mathbf{D}_s X_r\) solves:

\[ \mathrm{d}Y_r = \mu_x (r,X_r)Y_r \mathrm{d}r + \sigma_x (r,X_r) Y_r \mathrm{d}W_r, \quad Y_s = \sigma(s,X_s) \]

with explicit geometric solution:

\[ Y_t = \sigma(s,X_s)\exp\left\{\int_s^t \left(\mu_x (r,X_r) - \frac{1}{2} \sigma_{x}^2(r,X_r)\right)\mathrm{d}r + \int_s^t \sigma_x (r,X_r) \mathrm{d}W_r\right\} \]

Theorem 1 (Clark-Ocone Formula) Let \(F \in \mathbb{D}^{1,2}\) be \(\mathcal{F}_T\)-measurable. Then \(F\) admits the stochastic integral representation (Hairer 2021; Nualart 2019):

\[ F = \mathbb{E}[F] + \int_0^T \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{D}_t F \mid \mathcal{F}_t]\mathrm{d}W_t \]

Geometric and Manifold Considerations

Key geometric insights regarding this calculus include:

  1. Stationary Conditions vs. Optimization Geometry: While necessary conditions for stationary points can be derived heuristically, equipping the space of diffusion processes with a Riemannian manifold structure (enabling gradient descent) remains difficult.
  2. Variational Spaces: Variations on drift coefficients correspond naturally to the flat geometry of the Cameron-Martin space. However, variations on diffusion coefficients \(\sigma\) lack an obvious metric structure without restricting to specific density-space geometries (e.g., Wasserstein flows) (Lambert et al. 2022).

Explorations in Parametric & Wasserstein Flows

Parametric Perturbations

Consider a parameter \(\theta_t\) driving process \(X_t \sim P_{\theta_t}\) with Gaussian prior \(\theta_t \sim \mathcal{N}\left(\vartheta_t, \int_0^t v_s \mathrm{d}s\right)\). Representing \(X_t = G^{-1}(t, U, \theta_t)\) via conditional CDF \(G\):

\[ \mathbf{D}_s X_t = \left \langle \frac{\sigma_t \mathbb{I}_{[0,t]}(s)}{g(G^{-1}(t,U, \theta_t))}, \dot{\gamma}_s \right \rangle \]

In Wasserstein geometry, vector fields take the form \(V(\rho) = -\nabla \frac{\delta \mathcal{F}}{\delta \rho}\) (Lambert et al. 2022). For relative entropy \(\mathcal{F}[\rho] = \mathcal{D}_{KL}(\rho \parallel \rho^*)\), \(V(\rho) = -\left(\frac{\nabla \rho}{\rho} + \nabla f\right)\), corresponding to Langevin diffusion:

\[ \mathrm{d}X_t = -\nabla f (X_t) \mathrm{d}t + \sqrt{2} \mathrm{d}W_t \]

The first variation process for Langevin diffusion evaluates to:

\[ \mathbf{D}_s X_t = \sqrt{2}\exp\left\{-\int_s^t \Delta f (X_r)\mathrm{d}r\right\} \]

This indicates that pathwise perturbations decay or amplify exponentially according to accumulated spatial curvature \(\Delta f(X_r)\) along the trajectory.

Applying Girsanov’s Theorem with change of measure \(Z_t = \frac{\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}P}\Big|_t\):

\[ \frac{1}{2} \langle \mathbf{D}_s V(X_t), (\mathbf{D}_s V)^* (X_t) Z_t\rangle = \left \langle - \partial_t \exp\left\{-\int_s^t (\Delta f (X_r) - \Delta f_r (r,X_r) )\mathrm{d}r\right\}, Z_t \right \rangle \]

Mathematical Foundations

Malliavin Operators on Wiener Space

Let \(\mathbb{F}_t\) denote the \(L^2\)-space of \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-adapted processes generated by Brownian motion (Hairer 2021).

Theorem 2 (Wiener Representation Theorem) Every \(F \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}_T, P)\) admits a unique representation (Di Nunno et al. 2009; Nualart 2019):

\[ F_T = \mathbb{E}[F_T] + \int_{0}^{T} \alpha^{F}_t \mathrm{d} W_{t} \]

where \(\alpha^{F}_t\) is an adapted \(L^2\)-predictable process.

Definition 1 (Malliavin Derivative) Let \(F: \Omega \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\) be a random variable, \(g \in L^2([0,T])\), and \(\gamma(t) = \int_0^t g(s) \mathrm{d}s\). The directional derivative of \(F\) along \(\gamma\) is:

\[ \mathbf{D}_\gamma F(\omega) = \lim_{\varepsilon \rightarrow 0} \frac{F(\omega + \varepsilon \gamma) - F(\omega)}{\varepsilon} \]

If there exists \(\psi \in L^2([0,T] \times \Omega)\) such that \(\mathbf{D}_\gamma F = \langle \psi, g \rangle_{L^2[0,T]}\), then \(F \in \mathbb{D}^{1,2}\) and \(\mathbf{D}_t F = \psi(t, \cdot)\).

Definition 2 (Skorohod Integral (Divergence Operator)) The Skorohod integral operator \(\delta\) is defined as the \(L^2\)-adjoint of the Malliavin derivative \(\mathbf{D}\) (Nualart 2019):

\[ \mathbb{E}[F \cdot \delta(u)] = \mathbb{E}\left[\langle \mathbf{D} F, u \rangle_{L^2[0,T]}\right], \quad \forall F \in \mathbb{D}^{1,2}, \, u \in \text{Dom}(\delta) \]

For adapted processes \(u_t\), \(\delta(u) = \int_0^T u_t \mathrm{d}W_t\) coincides with the Itô integral (Di Nunno et al. 2009). For simple non-adapted processes \(u = \sum_{i=1}^n F_i h_i\):

\[ \delta(u) = \sum_{i=1}^n F_i W(h_i) - \sum_{i=1}^n \langle \mathbf{D} F_i, h_i \rangle_{L^2[0,T]} \]

Hermite polynomials \(H_n(x)\) arise directly via repeated divergence operations: \(H_n(x) = \delta^n(1)\) (Gjessing et al. 1992). The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck generator \(\Delta = -\delta \mathbf{D}\) satisfies:

\[ \Delta H_n = -n H_n \]

Chaos Expansions & Wick Calculus

Iterated stochastic integrals \(I_n(f_n) = \int_0^T \dots \int_0^{t_2} f_n(t_1, \dots, t_n) \mathrm{d}W_{t_1} \dots \mathrm{d}W_{t_n}\) satisfy \(I_n(g^{\otimes n}) = \|g\|^n H_n\left(\frac{\int g \mathrm{d}W}{\|g\|}\right)\) (Gjessing et al. 1992; Nualart 2019).

Definition 3 (Wick Product) For chaos expansions \(X = \sum_{\alpha} x_\alpha H_\alpha\) and \(Y = \sum_{\beta} y_\beta H_\beta\), the Wick product is (Gjessing et al. 1992):

\[ X \diamond Y = \sum_{\gamma} \left(\sum_{\alpha + \beta = \gamma} x_\alpha y_\beta \right) H_{\gamma} \]

Using Wick products, the SDE \(\mathrm{d}X_t = f(X_t)\mathrm{d}t + \sigma(X_t)\mathrm{d}W_t\) transforms into a random ordinary differential equation (Di Nunno et al. 2009):

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} X_t = f(X_t) + \sigma(X_t) \diamond \dot{W}_t \]

Example 1 (Geometric Brownian Motion in Wick Form) Geometric Brownian Motion \(\mathrm{d}X_t = \mu X_t \mathrm{d}t + \sigma X_t \mathrm{d}W_t\) written in Wick ODE form:

\[ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t} X_t = X_t \diamond (\mu + \sigma \dot{W}_t) \]

has the exact solution:

\[ X_t = x_0 \exp(\mu t) \diamond \exp^{\diamond}(\sigma W_t) = x_0 \exp\left(\left(\mu - \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)t + \sigma W_t\right) \]
