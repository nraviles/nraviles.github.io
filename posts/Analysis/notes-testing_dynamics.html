The premise for these notes comes from a discussion with a friend where it was alleged that under the null hypothesis \(H_0\), p-values should be comparable to a simple random walk. After considering the following example, it was made clear numerically that this is not exactly the case and for a rather interesting reason (there is a significant auto-correlation structure, and a non-trivial dependence on current value).

Consider the sequence of random variables \(X_{\bullet} =(X_1, X_2, \ldots)\) where \(X_i \iid N(0,1)\), and it’s sequence of running sums \(\Sigma_{\bullet} = (\Sigma_1, \Sigma_2, \ldots )\) with \(\Sigma_n = \sum_{i=1}^nX_i\). It is trivial to see that at each ``point in time’’, or rather addition of a new sample \(X_{n}\), with \(\rho_n = n^{-1/2}\) our z-scores \(Z_\bullet = \rho_\bullet \Sigma_\bullet = (\Sigma_1/\sqrt{1}, \Sigma_2/\sqrt{2}, \ldots)\) is marginally distributed \(N(0,1)\).

Consider the statistical test of \(H_0 : X_{i} \iid N(0,1)\) vs. for now an arbitrary alternative \(H_1:N(\mu,1), \ \mu \neq 0\). Under \(H_0\) as the distribution of \(Z_\bullet\) for any index is known, we have one again that marginally, where for our purposes consider \(\mcE(\cdot) = 2(1-\Pr[Z \le \abs{\cdot}]) = 2(1-\Phi (\cdot))\) with \(\Phi(\cdot)\) the CDF of a normal distribution and therefore \(E(\cdot)\) the p-value of our statistic (essentially twice the error function) so that in any case \[ \begin{equation*} \mcE\pr{\rho_\bullet \Sigma_\bullet} = U_\bullet = (U_1, U_2, \ldots), \quad U_i \sim \text{Unif}[0,1] \end{equation*} \] but where again \(U_i \not\ind U_j\) for \(i,j \in \Z_+\). We will importantly now consider the correlation structure of \(U_\bullet\), which is a fairly simply one in the SDE sense. We will first consider \(Z_\bullet\) as a discrete time process (as well as \(U_\bullet\)), and analyze the stochastic structure of \(U_\bullet\) via the standard argument of Taylor expansion (The non-linearity of \(E(\cdot)\) being the object that clearly induces a non-trivial correlation structure). It is reasonable to extend this discrete structure, instead as a discrete Taylor expansion, but we will side-step this for now; we may come back to this late. For simplicity, we will extend this analysis asymptotically in \(n\), and consider is extending \(Z_\bullet\) to the process with continuous time version.

Simple Continuous Time Case

Here we simplify our argument by consider \(\Sigma_t \doteq B_t = \int_{0}^t \dd B_t\) where \(B_t\) is a Brownian motion. We can therefore write via Ito’s lemma for \(Z_t = \rho_t B_t\) as \[ \begin{equation*} \dd \mcE(Z_t) = \partial_t \mcE(Z_t) \dd t + \partial_z\mcE(Z_t) \dd Z_t + \frac{\partial_z^2\mcE(Z_t)}{2}\dd \br{Z_t, Z_t}_t \end{equation*} \] Written explicitly in terms of \(Z_t\), we therefore have, with \(\partial_t \mcE(Z_t) = 0\) and \(\dd \br{Z_t, Z_t} = \dd \br{\rho_t B_t, \rho_t B_t} = \rho_t^2 \dd t\), \[ \begin{align*} \mcE(Z_t) &= \mcE(Z_1) + \int_{1}^t\dd \mcE(Z_t) \\ &= \mcE(Z_1) + \int_{1}^t \pr{\partial_t \mcE(Z_t) \dd t + \partial_z\mcE(Z_t) \dd Z_t + \frac{\partial_z^2\mcE(Z_t)}{2}\dd \br{Z_t, Z_t}_t} \\ &= \mcE(Z_1) + \int_{1}^t\partial_z\mcE(Z_t) \dd Z_t + \frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^t\rho_t^2\partial_z^2\mcE(Z_t) \dd t \end{align*} \]

Here we note that under \(H_0\) \(\mcE(Z_t)\sim U[0,1]\) at each time point, and therefore \[ \begin{align*} \frac{1}{2} =\E[\mcE(Z_t)] &= \E[\mcE(Z_1) + \int_{1}^t\partial_z\mcE(Z_t) \dd Z_t + \frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^t\rho_t^2\partial_z^2\mcE(Z_t) \dd t] \\ &= \frac{1}{2} + \E\br{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^t\rho_t^2\partial_z^2\mcE(Z_t) \dd t]} \end{align*} \] by definition, and therefore \(\E\br{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^t\rho_t^2\partial_z^2\mcE(Z_t) \dd t]} = 0\) for all \(t\); without assuming inter-changing the expectation and integral is valid.

Expanding out \(\dd Z_t\) properly, \[ \begin{align*} \dd Z_t &= \partial_t \rho_t B_t \dd t + \rho_t \dd B_t + \frac{1}{2}\dd \br{\rho_s, B_s}_t \\ &= \partial_t \rho_t B_t \dd t + \rho_t \dd B_t \\ &= \frac{\partial_t \rho_t}{\rho_t} Z_t \dd t + \rho_t \dd B_t , \end{align*} \] where the co-variation \(\dd \br{\rho_s, B_s}_t = 0\) as \(\rho_s\) is deterministic (finite variation, zero quadratic variation, increments have \(0\) covariance with \(B_s\); established later in discrete case as a useless fact - who is testing at infinity?). We observe \[ \begin{align*} \mcE\pr{Z_{t}} &= \mcE(Z_1) + \int_1^{t}\rho_{t}\partial \mcE(Z_t) \dd B_t + \int_{1}^{t}\pr{\frac{\partial_t \rho_t}{\rho_t} \partial \mcE(Z_t) Z_t + \frac{1}{2}\rho_{t}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_t)}\dd t \\ &= \mcE(Z_1) + \int_1^{t}\rho_{t}\partial \mcE(Z_t) \dd B_t + \int_{1}^{t}\pr{\partial_t \log\pr{ \rho_t} \partial \mcE(Z_t) Z_t + \frac{1}{2}\rho_{t}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_t)}\dd t. \end{align*} \]

This second term encompassing the drift is \[ \begin{equation*} \partial_t \log\pr{ \rho_t} \partial \mcE(Z_t)Z_t + \frac{1}{2}\rho_{t}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_t) = 2 Z_t \phi(Z_t)\pr{\partial_t \log\pr{ \rho_t} - \frac{1}{2}\rho_t^2} \end{equation*} \] expressed in terms of \(\phi(\cdot)\) the density of a standard normal as. We are interested now in exact or approximate solutions to \[ \begin{equation*} 2 Z_t \phi(Z_t)\pr{\partial_t \log\pr{ \rho_t} - \frac{1}{2}\rho_t^2} =0. \end{equation*} \] We observe that this implies the drift is \(0\) when \(Z_t = 0\), and or if the ODE \[ \begin{equation*} \partial_t \log\pr{ \rho_t} - \frac{1}{2}\rho_t^2 = 0 \end{equation*} \] is not satisfied. We know in this case the exact form of \(\rho_t\), but we can in any case solve this expression. This is a relatively simple ODE which can as previously identified just be expressed as \[ \begin{equation*} \partial_t \rho_t = \frac{1}{2}\rho_t^3 \end{equation*} \] providing a solution of \[ \begin{equation*} \rho_t = \frac{1}{\sqrt{C-t}} \end{equation*} \] for some constant \(C\), identifying a finite blow-up time.

We therefore observe, with \(\rho_t = t^{-1/2}\), that we only have approximate zeros. Firstly, \[ \begin{equation*} \partial_t \log\pr{ \rho_t} - \frac{1}{2}\rho_t^2 = -t \end{equation*} \] so that our expression of consideration (ha) is \[ \begin{equation*} -2t Z_t \phi(Z_t) \approx 0. \end{equation*} \] We observe two things (1) the sign of the drift is opposite in direction to \(Z_t\) (note: compared to its center, \(0\)), where with \(Z_t\) (in distribution) and \(\phi(\cdot)\) both symmetric we write \[ \begin{equation*} \bA_t \mcE(Z_t) = -2t\mathrm{sgn}(Z_t)\abs{Z_t}\phi(\abs{Z_t}) = -t \partial^2 \mcE(Z_t) \end{equation*} \] which is approximately zero only when \(\abs{Z_t}\approx 0\) or \(\abs{Z_t}\uparrow \infty\) (tending to \(0\) exponentially quickly when \(\abs{Z_t} \gg 1\)). Maximum drift at each \(t\) occurs when \(\abs{Z_t} = 1\) (when \(\abs{x}\phi(x)\) is at its peak), meaning that we should see the strongest reversion of our p-value process \(U_t\) back to \(1/2\) under the null when \(Z_t\approx1\).

Discrete Time Differences

A clear question now however, is how to relate \(U_i\) and \(U_j\) which will act as our sequential p-values. Under our prior assumptions, that the z-scores can be simply written in terms of the previous value as \[ \begin{align*} Z_{n} = Z_{n-1} + \pr{Z_{n} - Z_{n-1}} \doteq Z_{n} + \Delta Z_{n}. \end{align*} \] If we now express \(U_{n} = \mcE(Z_{n})\) in terms of \(Z_n\) via Taylor expansion, \[ \begin{align*} \mcE\pr{Z_{n}} &= \mcE\pr{ Z_n + \Delta Z_n} \\ &= \mcE( Z_n) + \partial \mcE(Z_n)\Delta Z_n+ \frac{1}{2}\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\Delta Z_n}^2 + R_n \end{align*} \] where explicitly \(R_n = \frac{1}{2}\pr{\partial^2 \mcE(\xi_n)-\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)}\pr{\Delta Z_n}^2\), where \(\xi_n\) by the mean value theorem is the midpoint corresponding to equality of our function value to the expansion.

As the \(\partial^3\mcE(\cdot)\) is also continuous and bounded, the remainder term \(R_n\) can be bounded in order. Note that we can express \[ \begin{align*} \Delta Z_n &= \rho_{n}\Sigma_{n} - \rho_{n-1}\Sigma_{n-1} \\ &= \rho_{n}\Delta\Sigma_{n} - \Delta \rho_{n}\Sigma_{n-1} \\ &\doteq \rho_{n}\Delta \Sigma_{n} + \frac{\Delta \rho_{n} }{\rho_{n-1}}Z_{n-1}. \end{align*} \] For large \(n\), remembering that \(\rho_n = 1/\sqrt{n}\), we see that \(\Delta\rho_{n} \approx -\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n\sqrt{n}}\), as \[ \begin{align*} \Delta \rho_{n} &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}} \\ &= \frac{\sqrt{n-1} - \sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{n}\sqrt{n-1}} \\ &= -\frac{1}{n\sqrt{n-1}}\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{n}}- \sqrt{1}}{ -\frac{1}{n}} \\ &\approx -\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n\sqrt{n-1}} = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n}\pr{\frac{\rho_{n-1}}{\rho_n}}\rho_n \end{align*} \] and therefore that \[ \begin{align*} \frac{\Delta \rho_{n} }{\rho_{n-1}}Z_{n-1} &\approx -\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n}\pr{1+\frac{1}{n}}^{1/2} Z_{n-1}, \quad n \uparrow \infty \\ &=O_p\pr{\frac{1}{n}}. \end{align*} \] Hence, the dominating factor here is \(\rho_n\Delta\Sigma_{n} =\rho_n X_{n} = O_p\pr{n^{-1/2}}\) in the difference \[ \begin{align*} \Delta Z_{n} &= \rho_{n}X_{n} + \frac{\Delta\rho_{n}}{\rho_{n-1}}Z_{n-1} = O_p\pr{n^{-1/2}} . \end{align*} \] Approximating the remainder (I believe it might just be Lipchitz, so maybe just a bound not approximation) \(\abs{\xi_n - Z_n} \le \abs{Z_{n} - Z_n}\) \[ \begin{align*} R_n &= \pr{\partial^2 \mcE(\xi_n)-\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)}\pr{\Delta Z_n}^2 \\ &\approx \partial^3 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\xi_n - Z_n}\pr{\Delta Z_n}^2 \\ &= O_p \pr{\abs{\Delta Z_{n} }^3} \\ &= O_p \pr{n^{-3/2}} \end{align*} \]

Asymptotic SDE

Using the expression \[ \begin{align*} \Delta Z_{n} & \doteq \rho_{n}\Delta \Sigma_{n} + \frac{\Delta \rho_{n} }{\rho_{n-1}}Z_n \end{align*} \] we can consolidate our Taylor expansion in terms of increments in \(\Sigma_n\) and \(\rho_n\) as \[ \begin{align*} \mcE\pr{Z_{n}} &= \mcE( Z_n) + \partial \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\rho_{n}\Delta \Sigma_{n} + \frac{\Delta \rho_{n} }{\rho_{n-1}}Z_n} + \frac{1}{2}\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\rho_{n}\Delta \Sigma_{n} + \frac{\Delta \rho_{n} }{\rho_{n-1}}Z_n}^2 + R_n \\ &= \mcE( Z_n) + \rho_{n}\partial \mcE(Z_n)\Delta \Sigma_{n} + \frac{\partial \mcE(Z_n) Z_n}{\rho_{n-1}}\Delta \rho_{n} + \frac{1}{2}\rho_{n}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\Delta \Sigma_{n}}^2 +\frac{1}{2}\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\frac{ Z_n}{\rho_{n-1}}}^2\pr{\Delta \rho_{n}}^2 + \tilde{R}_n \end{align*} \] where \(\tilde{R}_n\) includes not just the error but also the mixed term \(2\Delta\rho_{n}\Delta \Sigma_{n}Z_n\). We therefore arrive at a representation of the difference as \[ \begin{align*} \mcE\pr{Z_{n}} -\mcE( Z_n) = \rho_{n}&\partial \mcE(Z_n)\Delta \Sigma_{n} + \frac{\partial \mcE(Z_n) Z_n}{\rho_{n-1}}\Delta \rho_{n}\\ &+ \frac{1}{2}\rho_{n}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\Delta \Sigma_{n}}^2 +\frac{1}{2}\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\frac{ Z_n}{\rho_{n-1}}}^2\pr{\Delta \rho_{n}}^2 + \tilde{R}_n. \end{align*} \] Now, using these differences, by telescoping sum we see \[ \begin{align*} \mcE\pr{Z_{n}} = \mcE(Z_1) +\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho_{k}&\partial \mcE(Z_k)\Delta \Sigma_{k} + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\partial \mcE(Z_k) Z_k}{\rho_{k-1}}\Delta \rho_{k} \\ &+ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho_{k}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_k)\pr{\Delta \Sigma_{k}}^2 +\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\frac{Z_k}{\rho_{k-1}}}^2\pr{\Delta \rho_{k}}^2 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tilde{R}_n \end{align*} \]

We can see this also rise from the discrete time equation, where we let the increments in time \(\Delta t \downarrow 0\), and use an integral approximation of \[ \begin{align*} \mcE\pr{Z_{n}} = \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho_{k}&\partial \mcE(Z_k)\Delta \Sigma_{k} + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{\partial \mcE(Z_k) Z_k}{\rho_{k-1}}\Delta \rho_{k} \\ &+ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho_{k}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_k)\pr{\Delta \Sigma_{k}}^2 +\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\partial^2 \mcE(Z_n)\pr{\frac{ Z_k}{\rho_{k-1}}}^2\pr{\Delta \rho_{k}}^2 + \sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tilde{R}_k. \end{align*} \] We already showed that \(R_n = O_p\pr{\abs{Z_{n} - Z_n}^3}\). For our purposes in considering the continuous limit \(\tilde{R}_n\) is not so much changed given it adds a term of \(2 \Delta \Sigma_{n}\Delta\rho_{n} Z_n\) to \(R_n\), as the quadratic variation of \([Z,Z]_t = \rho_t^2 t\), where mixed term in the limit which is essentially \(2\dd \br{\int Z_{s} \dd B_s, \rho_s}_t=0\) is eventually negligible. The summation of \(R_k\) therefore tends to \(0\) and so \[ \begin{align*} \mcE\pr{Z_{t}} = \mcE(Z_1) + \int_1^{t}\rho_{t}&\partial \mcE(Z_t) \dd B_t + \int_{1}^{t}\pr{\frac{\partial_t \rho_t}{\rho_t} Z_t\partial \mcE(Z_t) + \frac{1}{2}\rho_{t}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_t)}\dd t. \end{align*} \] We see of course that this would be the same result as we would see from considering the Ito integral with respect to increments \[ \begin{align*} \dd Z_t &= \partial_t \rho_t B_t \dd t + \rho_t \dd B_t + \frac{1}{2}\dd \br{\rho_s, B_s}_t \\ &= \partial_t \rho_t B_t \dd t + \rho_t \dd B_t \\ &= \frac{\partial_t \rho_t}{\rho_t} Z_t \dd t + \rho_t \dd B_t . \end{align*} \] where the co-variation \(\dd \br{\rho_s, B_s}_t = 0\) as \(\rho_s\) is deterministic (finite variation, zero quadratic variation, increments have \(0\) covariance with \(B_s\)). This second term encompassing the drift is \[ \begin{equation} \frac{\partial_t \rho_t}{\rho_t} Z_t\partial \mcE(Z_t) + \frac{1}{2}\rho_{t}^2\partial^2 \mcE(Z_t). \end{equation} \]
